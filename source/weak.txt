@weak You confused "policy" with "optimal policy"
A: Your quiz feedback: you picked an answer implying a policy always chooses the best action. A policy is **any** mapping from states to actions, deterministic or stochastic, good or bad. Only $\pi_*$ is guaranteed to be best. If an examiner hears this mistake in your first two minutes, they will probe every definition after it. Drill the definition until you say "any strategy" without thinking.

@weak Symbol meanings that were slightly off
A: - "$\gamma$ is future rewards". No: $\gamma$ is the **discount factor** that weights future rewards.
- "$v_\pi$ is how much reward we get". It is the **expected discounted return**, an average over many futures, not one reward.
- "Deterministic: when you can only take one type of action in a state". A deterministic **policy** picks one action with probability 1. The state can still have many available actions.
- "Off-policy: as it updates its behavioural policy, it eventually updates the target policy". Backwards. Learning targets the **target** policy. In Q-learning the behaviour policy is derived from the same Q table, so it changes as a side effect.

@tip How to structure every answer
A: Use a four-beat answer and stop:
1. **Definition** in one sentence with the keyword.
2. **Intuition** or example (cliff walking, driving home, Blackjack).
3. **Equation**, if one exists, and name each symbol.
4. **Trade-off** or when you would not use it.
Thirty to sixty seconds. Then let them ask the next question. Long rambling answers give the examiner more places to catch errors.

@tip When you do not know
A: Say what you do know and reason out loud: "I am not certain, but from the Bellman equation I would expect...". Examiners grade reasoning. Never bluff a formula. Ask for clarification if the question is ambiguous, for example "do you mean on-policy prediction or control?".

@tip Comparison questions are the most likely
A: Oral examiners love "compare X and Y". Have a three-point comparison ready for: MC vs TD, SARSA vs Q-learning, policy iteration vs value iteration, on-policy vs off-policy, value-based vs policy-based, REINFORCE vs actor-critic, TRPO vs PPO, model-based vs model-free, first-visit vs every-visit, ordinary vs weighted importance sampling, experience replay vs target network. Points to cover each time: what it learns, bias and variance, data needed, when to choose it.
